## E1. Role count: pure RBAC vs RBAC + attributes (Kuhn, Coyne, Weil)

## E5. Password hashing cost (NIST SP 800-63B)

| Scheme | Time per guess (1 core) | Guesses per second an attacker with a stolen database can try (1 core) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Salted SHA-256 (old) | 0.21 us | 4,722,772 |
| PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, 600,000 iterations (now) | 136 ms | 7.4 |

Slow-down factor: about **641,358x** per guess. A person signing in waits one hash (the figure above); an attacker testing a billion guesses pays it a billion times. GPUs and ASICs are far faster than one CPU core for both schemes, so the ratio matters more than the absolute rate.

Blocklist: 533 common passwords bundled. Examples refused: `password123`, `Password1!`, `student123`, full-width Unicode variants.

## E6. Effect of the sign-in lockout

An attacker who guesses as fast as the lock allows can make **56 guesses against one account in 24 hours** (first 4 are free, then locks of 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 minutes, capped at 30 minutes). Without a limit, a script making 1,000 attempts per second would make 86,400,000.

The lock is stored in the database, so restarting the app does not reset it, and it also applies to usernames that do not exist (no account enumeration).
